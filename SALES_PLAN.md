# 📈 CLARO: Sales Plan & Strategy

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** September 2024  
**Status:** Sales Ready  
**Target Market:** Mid-Market & Enterprise HR Leaders

## Executive Summary

CLARO's sales strategy focuses on acquiring high-value mid-market and enterprise customers through a consultative sales approach. By combining inbound leads from marketing, strategic partnerships, and direct outreach, we'll build a scalable sales engine that achieves $500K ARR in Year 1 and $2M+ ARR in Year 2.

**Sales Vision:**
"Help organizations prevent burnout by arming managers with the tools, training, and insights they need to recognize and act on warning signs before crises occur."

## Part 1: Sales Strategy Overview

### Sales Model

**Hybrid Sales Model:**
1. **Inbound (40% of revenue)**
   - Leads from marketing campaigns
   - Website inquiries and trials
   - Content downloads and webinars
   - Search engine marketing
   - Self-qualified prospects

2. **Partnerships (30% of revenue)**
   - EAP providers referrals
   - HR tech platform integrations
   - Benefits broker channel
   - Consultant recommendations
   - Co-selling opportunities

3. **Enterprise Direct (20% of revenue)**
   - Executive outreach campaigns
   - Account-based marketing (ABM)
   - Custom solution selling
   - High-touch relationship management
   - Multi-threaded deals

4. **Community & Referrals (10% of revenue)**
   - Customer reference program
   - Industry community engagement
   - Word-of-mouth referrals
   - Case study-driven inbound
   - Expansion within existing customers

### Sales Approach

**Consultative Selling Model:**
- Start with discovery of actual pain points
- Diagnose burnout challenges in their organization
- Present CLARO as solution to specific problems
- Build consensus among stakeholders
- Demonstrate ROI with customized analysis
- Support implementation and success

**Not:** Feature-dump, transactional, volume-based

### Sales Cycle & Timeline

**Average Sales Cycle: 6-8 weeks**

| Stage | Duration | Activities |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| **Discovery** | Week 1-2 | Initial call, needs assessment, sponsor identification |
| **Qualification** | Week 2-3 | Budget verification, timeline alignment, stakeholder mapping |
| **Evaluation** | Week 3-5 | Free trial, demonstrations, ROI analysis, reference calls |
| **Proposal** | Week 5-6 | Custom proposal, pricing negotiation, legal review |
| **Closing** | Week 6-8 | Contract execution, implementation kickoff, success planning |

**Triggers for acceleration:**
- Specific retention crisis
- Manager training budget cycle
- Wellness initiative mandate from board/CEO
- New HR technology investment season
- Post-acquisition integration needs

## Part 2: Target Customer Profiles

### Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

#### Organization Characteristics
- **Size:** 250-2,000 employees (mid-market); 2,000+ (enterprise)
- **Industries:** Tech, Finance, Healthcare, Professional Services, Manufacturing, Consulting
- **Geography:** US primary; UK, Canada secondary
- **Growth Stage:** Scaling, post-funding, or stable enterprise
- **Maturity:** Modern HR Tech stack (Workday, ADP, SuccessFactors)

#### Pain Points (Priority Order)
1. **Turnover crisis** ($150K-500K per person, 20%+ annual rate)
2. **Manager capabilities gap** (limited training on mental health)
3. **Wellness program utilization** (<30% EAP usage, siloed wellness)
4. **Burnout visibility** (no way to identify at-risk employees until departure)
5. **Culture transformation** (reactive vs. preventive approach)
6. **ROI justification** (hard to prove wellness impact)

#### Buying Motivations
- New CHRO or Head of People (12-month window to implement)
- Board mandate on employee mental health
- Retention crisis prompting urgent action
- Post-layoff culture recovery initiative
- Competitive talent war requiring wellness differentiator
- Annual wellness program review/budget cycle

#### Buying Committee
- **Primary Sponsor:** Chief People Officer (CHRO) or Head of HR
- **Executive Champion:** CEO, Chief Operating Officer
- **Influencer:** Head of Organizational Development, Benefits Director
- **User Champion:** Manager of managers, department heads
- **Budget Owner:** VP Finance, CFO (via business case)

#### Budget & Timeline
- **Decision authority:** CHRO or C-level (typically $50K+ spending)
- **Budget cycle:** Often Jan-Mar or Sept-Nov (annual planning)
- **Contract process:** 2-4 weeks legal review (enterprise)
- **Implementation timeline:** Target Q1, Q3, or after annual planning

### Secondary Customer Profiles

#### Small Mid-Market (100-250 employees)
- Faster decision, smaller deal size ($10-15K)
- Higher propensity to adopt
- Potential for expansion
- Lower CAC through self-serve/freemium

#### Managed HR Services (HR Departments selling to clients)
- ADP, Mercer, Willis Towers Watson, BrightHire
- Looking for new offerings to customers
- Revenue share model (10-20%)
- White-label potential

#### EAP & Mental Health Providers
- Spring Health, Ginger, Talkspace, providers
- Seeking prevention layer to crisis response
- Integration or partnership model
- Potential acquisition target

## Part 4: Sales Process & Methodology

### Stage 1: Discovery & Qualification (Weeks 1-3)

**Objective:** Understand if prospect is qualified and if CLARO is right fit

**Key Activities:**
- Initial consultation call (30 min)
- Needs assessment questionnaire
- Sponsor/champion identification
- Current state analysis (burnout challenges, manager capabilities, tools)
- Budget and timeline alignment
- Stakeholder mapping

**Success Criteria (Go/No-Go):**
- ✅ Organization size 250+ employees
- ✅ HR tech budget available ($10-50K annually)
- ✅ Primary sponsor identified (CHRO, Head of People)
- ✅ Timeline within 6-8 weeks
- ✅ Problem/pain point clearly articulated
- ❌ No-go: Too small, no budget, wrong decision maker, no timeline

**Sales Collateral:**
- Introduction one-pager
- Qualification scorecard
- Needs assessment template
- Internal evaluation checklist

### Stage 2: Evaluation & Demonstration (Weeks 3-5)

**Objective:** Show CLARO's value and build consensus with buying committee

**Key Activities:**
- Customize free trial (upload sample data if possible)
- Detailed product demo (focus on their pain points)
- Manager training overview
- Implementation timeline presentation
- Reference customer call(s) - similar company/size
- ROI analysis and business case development
- Q&A and objection handling

**Decision Makers Present:**
- CHRO or Head of People (must attend)
- Head of Organizational Development (ideal)
- Department manager (for user perspective)
- CFO or Finance representative (for budget)

**Sales Collateral:**
- Customized product demo deck
- Case study(ies) - similar organization
- ROI calculator and business case template
- Implementation timeline with success metrics
- Customer testimonial video(s)
- Technical specification sheet
- Pricing sheet with terms

### Stage 3: Proposal & Negotiation (Weeks 5-6)

**Objective:** Formalize solution and price with buy-in from all stakeholders

**Key Activities:**
- Deliver formal proposal with:
  - Executive summary (1 page)
  - Current state analysis
  - Proposed solution (features, timeline)
  - ROI justification and financial impact
  - Implementation plan with milestones
  - Pricing, terms, and support
  - Customer success metrics and KPIs
- Present proposal in stakeholder meeting
- Address questions and concerns
- Negotiate terms if needed (payment schedule, add-ons, discounts)
- Legal review and contract negotiation

**Pricing Negotiation Guidelines:**
- **Standard discount:** 10% for annual prepay
- **Volume discount:** 15% for $75K+ annual spend
- **Multi-year discount:** 10% off Year 2-3 if 3-year commitment
- **Partner channel:** 20% discount (margin for partners)
- **Limits:** No more than 20% discount without executive approval

**Contract Length:**
- Standard: 12 months
- Preferred: 3 years (10% discount)
- Minimum: Annual with quarterly true-ups for growth

**Sales Collateral:**
- Formal proposal document
- Executive summary (1-pager)
- Implementation timeline
- Support and success plan
- Terms and conditions
- Data security/compliance summary

### Stage 4: Closing & Signature (Weeks 6-8)

**Objective:** Execute contract and transition to customer success

**Key Activities:**
- Final negotiations and adjustments
- Executive sign-off (CEO/President for $50K+)
- Contract execution (digital signature)
- Initial kick-off call
- Success metrics alignment
- Payment setup
- Resource assignment for implementation

**Red Flags / Stalled Deals:**
- Long legal review (>4 weeks) → Escalate to legal/CEO
- Budget questions → Provide ROI justification
- Stakeholder misalignment → Re-align with sponsor
- Contract delays → Weekly follow-up calls
- Cold radio → Reach out to original champion

**Sales Collateral:**
- Final contract (signed template)
- Implementation kickoff agenda
- Customer success team introduction
- Training schedule
- Success metrics dashboard
- Executive sponsor welcome letter

## Part 5: Key Sales Conversations & Objection Handling

### Discovery Call Script (30 minutes)

**Opening:**
"Thanks for taking the time to meet. The reason I reached out is we work with organizations to help them recognize and prevent burnout before it costs them people. I'd love to understand what's happening in your organization and if this resonates."

**Discovery Questions:**
1. "What's the biggest challenge you're facing with employee retention right now?"
2. "How aware are your managers of burnout warning signs in their teams?"
3. "What tools do you currently have to identify at-risk employees?"
4. "How do you measure the success of your wellness programs today?"
5. "What would prevention of a burnout-related resignation be worth to your organization?"
6. "What's your timeline for exploring solutions in this space?"

**Closing:**
"Based on what you've shared, I think we could help. Let me put together some information specific to your situation and we can explore if it makes sense to do a pilot with a few teams."

### Common Objections & Responses

**Objection 1: "We already have an EAP program."**

Response: "EAPs are great for crisis response—but they're reactive. Most people wait until things are really bad before reaching out. CLARO is about prevention. It helps your managers identify burnout early—at the 30-50 burnout score—before it reaches crisis. Think of it like this: EAP treats the fire, CLARO prevents the fire."

Data to share:
- 60% more effective when addressed early
- Only 30% of employees use EAP
- Prevention costs $5-10K vs. replacement cost of $150-300K

---

**Objection 2: "This sounds like surveillance of our employees."**

Response: "I totally understand that concern, and it's important to us too. CLARO is built on transparency and employee choice. Employees complete their own assessment—they're choosing to share their data. Managers get insights only if employees opt in. It's not monitoring—it's giving managers the awareness and skills to have supportive conversations. Many of our customers make this a key talking point in employee communications."

Key points:
- Voluntary assessment
- Employee data control
- Manager training focuses on support (not punishment)
- Privacy-first architecture

---

**Objection 3: "How is this different from general wellness apps like Headspace?"**

Response: "Headspace is great for daily wellness, but burnout isn't solved by meditation alone. Burnout comes from organizational factors—workload, culture, lack of growth. Meditation can help with stress, but it doesn't change the real problem. CLARO is designed specifically for burnout. It gives managers the tools to actually address the root causes—helping with workload, building culture, creating growth opportunities. It's prevention + tools + accountability."

Key differences to highlight:
- Burnout-specific assessment
- Manager accountability + tools
- Organizational culture focus
- Measurable ROI

---

**Objection 4: "We're not sure about budget for this."**

Response: "That's fair. Let me share the ROI math. The average cost to replace someone is 150% of salary. At your company with 500 people, if you're saving just one resignation from burnout, that's $150-300K in costs avoided. Most of our customers see 5-10 preventions in the first year. That means $750K-3M in direct cost savings—not counting improved retention and productivity. Given that, the $35K annual investment has a payback in the first 2-3 months. Would it be worth doing a small pilot to test that?"

ROI conversation:
- Cost of one replacement: $150K+ (100-150% salary)
- Average preventions Year 1: 5-10
- Direct ROI: $750K-1.5M
- Payback period: 2-3 months
- Plus productivity, engagement, retention improvements

---

**Objection 5: "We need to check with legal/security/compliance."**

Response: "Absolutely—that's standard. We have SOC 2 Type II compliance, data is encrypted, and we comply with HIPAA and GDPR. I can get you our security documentation, privacy policy, and data processing agreement right away. Most legal reviews take 2-3 weeks once they have that. Let's get those documents out today so we're not held up by admin."

Security points to emphasize:
- SOC 2 Type II compliance
- Data encryption at rest and in transit
- HIPAA and GDPR compliant
- Regular security audits
- DPA (Data Processing Agreement) available
- Employee data control and transparency

Does this resonate with your situation?"
